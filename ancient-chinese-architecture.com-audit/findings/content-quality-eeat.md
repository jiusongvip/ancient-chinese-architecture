### Experience (8/25) — Weak

**What works:**
- Content demonstrates genuine familiarity with architectural terminology (dougong, cai-fen, xumizuo, siheyuan, hangtu)
- Building descriptions include precise details that suggest research (980 buildings, 72 hectares, 857 CE, 67 meters, 54 bracket types)
- Travel tips are practical and specific ("Book Forbidden City tickets 7 days ahead", "Enter through the Meridian Gate")

**What's missing:**
- No first-person narrative or personal experience signals at all
- All images are from Unsplash (stock) — no original photography despite the travel-guide aspect
- No "I visited..." / "I tested..." / "In my experience..." framing
- No original research, case studies, or proprietary data
- The site reads as a well-researched encyclopedia rather than someone sharing lived experience

**Why this matters (Dec 2025 update):** Experience is the dimension AI cannot fabricate. Sites without first-hand experience signals are increasingly at risk under the December 2025 core update.

---

### Expertise (18/25) — Moderate-Strong

**What works:**
- Strong demonstrated knowledge of Chinese architectural history
- Accurate use of specialized vocabulary (dougong bracket systems, cai-fen modular timber, zoushou ridge beasts, wudian/xieshan roof forms)
- Content covers multiple dimensions: structural engineering, historical context, symbolic meaning, practical travel information
- Technical claims are generally accurate and specific
- Depth appropriate for educated general audience

**What's missing:**
- No author credentials, certifications, or bio
- No bylines on any page
- No "About the Author" section
- Sources not cited — while content appears accurate, readers cannot verify claims

**Verdict:** The expertise is clearly present in the content, but it's disembodied expertise. Google's raters cannot verify who wrote it or what qualifies them. This is the "ghost expert" problem.

---

### Authoritativeness (5/25) — Very Weak

**What works:**
- Consistent brand identity ("Ancient Chinese Architecture")
- Clean, professional design that conveys seriousness
- Domain name is descriptive and appropriate
- Content covers a coherent topical niche

**What's missing:**
- Zero external citations or backlinks visible
- No brand mentions, industry recognition, or awards
- Author(s) not recognized as experts in any external context
- No professional affiliations (universities, museums, architectural societies)
- No publication history beyond this site
- No references or bibliography

**Verdict:** This is a self-contained authority island — knowledgeable but unvalidated. Building external authority requires outreach: guest posts, academic citations, Wikipedia references, partnerships with museums or universities.

---

### Trustworthiness (12/30) — Weak-Moderate

**What works:**
- HTTPS (via astro.config.mjs site declaration)
- Clean, transparent design with no deceptive elements
- No clickbait, hidden ads, or affiliate links
- Content is factually consistent across pages
- Footer includes copyright and domain name
- About page explains the project's mission

**What's missing:**
- No contact information (no email, no physical address)
- No privacy policy or terms of service
- No date stamps on content (no publication or last-updated dates)
- No author identity for accountability
- Reviews, testimonials, or social proof absent
- No correction/update history visible

**Verdict:** The site doesn't actively deceive, but it doesn't actively build trust either. For an educational resource about cultural heritage — a topic where accuracy matters — the lack of contact info, author identity, and date stamps undermines credibility.

## AI Citation Readiness: 45/100

### Quotability Assessment

**What works:**
- Building pages contain highly quotable data points:
  - "980 buildings across 72 hectares" (Forbidden City)
  - "67 meters tall — oldest all-wood pagoda in the world" (Yingxian Pagoda)
  - "857 CE" (Foguang Temple East Hall)
  - "Second-oldest surviving timber-frame building in China"
  - "54 different dougong bracket types" (Yingxian Pagoda)
- Content uses clear definition patterns (dynasty summaries, element definitions)
- FAQ page follows Q&A format ideal for AI extraction
- FAQPage JSON-LD schema now present (added in our fix)

**What's missing:**
- No llms.txt or llms-full.txt for LLM crawler guidance
- No paragraph-level anchors for fragment URL citation
- No author metadata in schema → LLMs have no one to credit
- No first-party data or original statistics
- FAQ answers are short (47-57 words) — AI prefers 100-200 word comprehensive answers

**AI Search Visibility Strategy:**
The site has strong foundational quotability for its core factual claims. Adding llms.txt, extending FAQ answers, and adding author Person schema would significantly improve AI citation readiness. Cross-reference the `seo-geo` skill for detailed GEO workflows.

## Recommendations (Priority Order)

### Critical (December 2025 Core Update Impact)
1. **Add author identity:** Create an "About the Author" page or section with credentials, background, and expertise in Chinese architecture. Add Person schema.
2. **Add date stamps:** Show publication dates and last-updated dates on all content pages.

### High Impact
3. **Create actual blog posts:** The 3 previewed articles (Dougong Mathematics, Tang Temple Ruins, Feng Shui) should be real pages with Article schema, author attribution, and citations.
4. **Add content to Compare page:** The dynasty comparison has almost no indexable text — it's all JavaScript-rendered. Add server-side comparison content.
5. **Extend dynasty detail pages:** The dynasty pages feel like index entries rather than destination pages. Add architectural examples, diagrams, and deeper historical context.

### Medium Impact
6. **Add bibliography/sources section** to building and dynasty pages
7. **Replace Unsplash images** with original photography or properly licensed architectural images with alt text
8. **Add contact information** and privacy policy

### Low Impact
9. **Extend FAQ answers** to 100-200 words each for better AI extraction
10. **Add table of contents** to long building detail pages

---

## Conclusion

The site demonstrates genuine expertise — the content is accurate, well-structured, and covers its niche thoroughly. But it's expertise without a face. The single most impactful change: **put a name and credentials behind the content.** Everything else (citations, dates, original photography) builds on that foundation.

**Estimated E-E-A-T score after fixes:** 65-75/100 (from current 43/100)
